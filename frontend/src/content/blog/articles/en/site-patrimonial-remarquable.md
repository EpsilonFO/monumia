Your house is neither among the listed (classé) properties nor among the registered (inscrit) ones, and yet the town hall tells you that your façade works must go through a special opinion. This is often a sign that your property lies within a **Site Patrimonial Remarquable** (SPR, an outstanding heritage site). This perimeter protects an entire neighbourhood or village, even if each building is not individually protected. Here is what it means for you in practice.

## At a glance

The SPR is a **protection perimeter** covering a town, village or neighbourhood of heritage interest. It does not protect a single building, but a coherent whole. If your property is within this perimeter, any works altering its external appearance require the **agreement of the Architecte des Bâtiments de France** (ABF, the state heritage architect), even if your house is neither listed nor registered. Routine maintenance, however, remains unrestricted.

## What is a Site Patrimonial Remarquable?

The Site Patrimonial Remarquable was created by the **law of 7 July 2016** on freedom of creation, architecture and heritage. It replaced three earlier schemes that you may still come across in old documents: **secteurs sauvegardés** (safeguarded sectors), **ZPPAUP** and **AVAP** (two earlier types of architectural and heritage protection zone).

In practice, an SPR aims to protect towns, villages or neighbourhoods ‘whose conservation, restoration, rehabilitation or enhancement is of public interest’ from a historical, architectural, archaeological, artistic or landscape point of view. Rural areas or landscapes can also be included when they form a coherent whole with the built environment.

This is what is known as a **servitude d’utilité publique** (a public-interest easement): a constraint imposed by the public authorities in the general interest, which applies to every owner within the perimeter. France now has more than **860 sites** designated in this way.

:::remarque
Being in an SPR is **not the same thing** as being listed or registered as a historic monument. The protection applies to the **urban ensemble**, not to your property in particular. A property can, moreover, hold both statuses. For the distinction between listed and registered, see our article [Listed or registered: what’s the difference?](/blog/article/classe-ou-inscrit).
:::

## PSMV or PVAP: the plan that governs your area

Each SPR is governed by a **management document** that sets the rules for works. There are two types, and knowing which one applies to you changes what you can do.

:::comparatif{a="PSMV" b="PVAP"}
- Full name | Plan de sauvegarde et de mise en valeur (safeguarding and enhancement plan) | Plan de valorisation de l’architecture et du patrimoine (architecture and heritage enhancement plan)
- Legal framework | Code de l’urbanisme (planning code) | Code du patrimoine (heritage code)
- Level of detail | The most protective | Enhancement rules
- Building interiors | Can be regulated | Not covered, in principle
- Malraux tax relief | 30% reduction | 22% reduction
:::

The same SPR can combine both tools in different areas. These plans can be **consulted at the town hall** or on your municipality’s website: it is the first document to ask for before any project.

## What it changes for your works

Within an SPR, all works likely to **alter the external appearance** of a property (façade, roof, windows and doors, shopfront), as well as works on unbuilt spaces (courtyard, garden), require **prior authorisation**. Where a PSMV applies, some **interior** works may also be affected.

This authorisation is issued by your town hall, but only **with the agreement of the Architecte des Bâtiments de France** (ABF). The ABF, who works within the Unité départementale de l’architecture et du patrimoine (UDAP, the département’s architecture and heritage office), makes sure your project fits well into its surroundings. Their agreement may come with **requirements** (choice of a material, a colour, a technique).

:::attention[Routine maintenance remains unrestricted]
Repainting in the same colour, replacing a tile with a matching one, maintaining a wall: these routine maintenance tasks **do not require** authorisation. It is **altering** the external appearance that triggers the process. If in doubt, a call to the UDAP will save you from having your works halted.
:::

Here is how a typical application unfolds:

:::etapes
1. **Consult the plan**: PSMV or PVAP, at the town hall, to find out the rules for your area.
2. **Contact the UDAP**: early on, to scope your project with the ABF before finalising quotes.
3. **Submit your application**: prior declaration (déclaration préalable) or building permit (permis de construire), depending on the scale of the works.
4. **Wait for the ABF’s opinion**: their agreement is a condition of the town hall’s permission.
:::

Processing times are longer than in an ordinary area: generally allow up to **2 months** for a prior declaration and up to **3 or 4 months** for a building permit. If the ABF refuses, an **appeal** can be made to the regional prefect (préfet de région).

## What are the benefits for owners?

The constraints of the SPR come with something in return. Restoration works on a building located in an SPR can qualify for the **Malraux scheme**, an income tax reduction calculated on restoration expenditure. The rate depends on the plan that applies to your area:

:::barres{unit="%"}
- SPR covered by an approved PSMV: 30
- SPR without an approved PSMV: 22
:::

This benefit is subject to conditions (in particular a commitment to let the property, for the buildings concerned) and is capped. As tax rules change, consult an adviser or your tax office to check your eligibility before committing.

:::astuce
The right reflex: book an appointment with the **UDAP** as soon as you start thinking about works, before signing a quote. The ABF is a source of support, not just a check: they can steer you towards the right technical solutions and help you avoid a refusal.
:::

## A worked example

Monsieur Lopez owns a town house in the historic centre of a municipality designated as an SPR, **covered by an approved PSMV**. He wants to restore the façade and replace the windows: a quote for €60,000 of eligible restoration works.

:::chiffres
- **€60,000**: amount of the restoration works
- **30%**: applicable Malraux rate (area covered by a PSMV)
- **~€18,000**: potential tax reduction
:::

First of all, he consults the PSMV at the town hall, then meets the ABF at the UDAP, who approves the project while requiring wooden windows in keeping with the character of the neighbourhood. Monsieur Lopez submits a prior declaration, obtains the ABF’s agreement, then carries out the works. Provided he meets the conditions of the Malraux scheme, his restoration could entitle him to a tax reduction of around €18,000.

## Key takeaways

- The **SPR** protects an urban or landscape ensemble, not an isolated building.
- Your external works there are **regulated**, even if your property is neither listed nor registered.
- The **PSMV** or the **PVAP** sets the rules: consult it at the town hall.
- The **ABF’s agreement** is a condition of your works authorisation; routine maintenance remains unrestricted.
- The SPR can open up the **Malraux** tax benefit (22% or 30% depending on the plan).

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find out whether my property is in an SPR?**
Ask your town hall or the UDAP in your département. The perimeter, set by order, can be consulted at the town hall along with the management plan.

**Who decides to create an SPR?**
The **designation** (classement) is made by order of the minister responsible for culture, after consulting the Commission nationale du patrimoine et de l’architecture (the national heritage and architecture committee) and holding a public inquiry, on the proposal or with the agreement of the municipality.

**Do I have to use an architect?**
It depends on the scale of the works and your municipality’s rules. An architect is required for projects needing a building permit above certain thresholds; the UDAP will advise you on your case.
