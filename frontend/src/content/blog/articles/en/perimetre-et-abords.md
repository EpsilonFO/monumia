Your house is neither listed (classé) nor registered (inscrit), and yet the town hall asks for a special opinion before you repaint your façade or change your windows. The reason? Your property lies ‘in the surroundings’ (aux abords) of a historic monument. This very common situation creates real obligations, even though your own property is not protected. Here is how to find out whether you are affected and what it changes for your works.

## At a glance

The **surroundings** (abords) are a protection zone around a historic monument. If your property lies within them, any works that alter its external appearance (façade, roof, windows and doors, fencing) must receive the agreement of the **Architecte des Bâtiments de France** (ABF, the state heritage architect). This agreement is requested at the same time as your planning application, submitted to the town hall. You remain the owner and free to use your property as you wish: only its visible appearance is regulated.

## What exactly are the ‘surroundings’?

The surroundings protect buildings that form **a coherent whole** with a historic monument, or that are likely to contribute to its conservation or enhancement. The idea is simple: a monument does not exist in isolation. Its immediate environment (the neighbouring buildings, the streets, the views) is part of how it is perceived.

In practice, two regimes currently coexist:

- The **périmètre délimité des abords** (PDA), a protected surroundings area drawn to measure around the monument. Created by the law of 7 July 2016, it is gradually replacing the old automatic perimeter.
- Where there is no PDA, the **default perimeter**: a **500-metre** radius around the monument, within which only properties in **intervisibility** (covisibilité) with it are affected.

:::comparatif{a="Default perimeter" b="Delimited perimeter (PDA)"}
- Shape | 500 m radius around the monument | Zone drawn to measure
- Properties affected | Those in intervisibility with the monument | All properties in the zone
- Intervisibility | Assessed case by case by the ABF | No longer needs proving: automatic protection
- Origin | Historical default regime | Law of 7 July 2016
:::

## Intervisibility: the test for the default perimeter

As long as no PDA has been established, protection does not apply to the whole 500-metre radius: it covers properties located **within the field of view** of the monument. This is the notion of **intervisibility**.

A property is in intervisibility when it is **visible from the monument**, or **visible at the same time as it** from a point accessible to the public. It is the ABF who assesses this link. Two neighbouring houses can therefore fall under different rules: one visible from the nearby bell tower, the other hidden behind a building.

:::remarque
The **périmètre délimité des abords** changes things: within it, intervisibility no longer has to be demonstrated. All properties in the zone are affected, whether or not they can see the monument. Ask at the town hall which regime applies to you.
:::

## Which works are affected?

The works concerned are those that **alter the external appearance** of a property located in the surroundings. For example:

- **resurfacing** the façade or changing its colour;
- replacing **windows, shutters or doors**;
- redoing the **roof** with a change of material;
- installing **solar panels**, a conservatory, a fence or a sign.

**Like-for-like maintenance**, which changes nothing about the appearance, is generally exempt from this formality. If in doubt, the right reflex is to ask the Unité départementale de l’architecture et du patrimoine (UDAP, the département’s architecture and heritage office), where the ABF works.

:::attention[A binding opinion]
In the surroundings, the ABF’s opinion is a **binding opinion** (avis conforme): the authority that issues your permission (most often the mayor) is required to follow it. A refusal from the ABF therefore blocks the project as it stands. It is better to consult the ABF **early**, before settling on your choice of materials.
:::

## How the process works

You do not contact the ABF directly for your permission: everything goes through your **planning application** submitted to the town hall, either a prior declaration (déclaration préalable) or a building permit (permis de construire) depending on the scale of the works. The town hall forwards the file to the ABF, who gives an opinion within set time limits.

:::etapes
1. **Check at the town hall**: find out whether the property is in the surroundings and under which regime (PDA or 500 m).
2. **Contact the UDAP early**: to scope the project with the ABF before finalising your choices.
3. **Submit the planning application**: prior declaration or building permit, at the town hall.
4. **Review by the ABF**: their agreement is a condition of the final permission.
:::

:::chiffres
- **500 m**: radius of the default perimeter around the monument
- **1 month**: time allowed for the ABF’s opinion on a prior declaration
- **2 months**: time allowed for the ABF’s opinion on a building permit
:::

## What if you disagree with the ABF?

An unfavourable opinion is not always final. In the event of disagreement, the applicant can ask the **mayor** to lodge an appeal with the **regional prefect** (préfet de région). This appeal must be made within a very short period (around **seven days** after receipt of the ABF’s opinion), and the matter is then examined by the commission régionale du patrimoine et de l’architecture (the regional heritage and architecture committee).

The regional prefect then issues an opinion that **replaces** the ABF’s. It is a useful route for dialogue, but it is governed by strict deadlines: do not delay.

## A worked example

Monsieur Da Silva owns a house in a small market town, **not protected**, 200 metres from a listed church. No PDA has yet been established in his municipality, so the default 500-metre perimeter applies. Since his façade is visible at the same time as the church from the village square, his property is in **intervisibility**.

:::exemple
He wants to replace his wooden windows with white PVC ones and install solar panels on the street-facing façade. Before signing a quote, he makes an appointment at the UDAP. The ABF suggests wood-effect windows and placing the panels on the garden side, out of sight from the square. The adjusted project receives a favourable opinion and his works start without a hitch.
:::

By planning ahead, Monsieur Da Silva avoided a refusal and an appeal. The lesson: in the surroundings, early dialogue with the ABF saves time.

## Key takeaways

- Being **in the surroundings** of a historic monument creates obligations, even if your property itself is **not protected**.
- Two regimes: the **périmètre délimité des abords** (drawn to measure) or, failing that, the **500-metre radius** with the **intervisibility** test.
- Only works **altering the external appearance** are affected; like-for-like maintenance generally remains unrestricted.
- The ABF’s opinion is a **binding opinion**: the town hall must follow it.
- In the event of disagreement, an **appeal to the regional prefect** is possible, within very short time limits.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find out whether my property is in the surroundings?**
Ask your town hall or consult the local development plan (plan local d’urbanisme): protection easements are shown on it. The UDAP for your département can also confirm which regime applies.

**My property is in the surroundings: is it a ‘historic monument’?**
No. Being in the surroundings does not protect your property as a historic monument. To understand the difference between the statuses, see [Listed or registered: what’s the difference?](/blog/article/classe-ou-inscrit).

**Are interior works affected?**
In principle, no: only works altering the visible external appearance require the ABF’s opinion.
